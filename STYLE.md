## Graphics and illustrations

Use a visual when it explains something better than another paragraph would, or when the article needs a deliberate change of pace. A figure should add a view of the subject rather than repeat the prose around it.

Use the shared components when they fit:

- `BarChart` / `LineChart` — numbers across categories or time.
- `DataTable` — compact facts that are easier to scan than read in a sentence.
- `Compare` — two to four things compared across the same attributes.
- `FlowSequence` — an ordered progression or pipeline.
- `FeedbackLoop` — a cycle where the return edge matters.
- `AnnotatedCode` — one snippet explained line by line.
- `NumberLine` — values positioned relative to each other or a boundary.
- `MemoryBoxes` — sharing, indirection, aliases, and boxed values.
- `DiffReview` — a code change with review comments attached to the relevant lines.
- `MessageThread` — a short exchange where the turn-taking is part of the point.
- `SocialPost` — a cited X or Bluesky post used as evidence.
- `ChoiceOverload` — one simple request facing a pile of interchangeable options.
- `ImageFigure` — photographs, artwork, screenshots, and the occasional GIF.

Keep bespoke graphics beside the article that uses them. Move one into the shared component library only after another article genuinely needs the same visual language.

Every article also has a hero image in its metadata. Prefer a freely licensed photograph or artwork, usually from Wikimedia Commons, and record the author, license, and source. Real objects, machines, tools, places, and older artwork tend to fit the site better than stock photography or generated art.

Give every image useful alt text and every figure a caption that says what the reader is looking at. The article should still make sense if the reader skips the visual entirely.
